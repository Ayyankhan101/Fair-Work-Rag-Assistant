Division 4—Unfair dismissal

385  What is an unfair dismissal

(1) A person has been unfairly dismissed if the FWC is satisfied that:
    (a) the person has been dismissed; and
    (b) the dismissal was harsh, unjust or unreasonable.

(2) However, the FWC is not satisfied that a person has been unfairly dismissed if:
    (a) the person was dismissed for a reason that is related to:
        (i) the conduct of the person; or
        (ii) the capacity of the person; and
    (b) the FWC is satisfied that the reason was a valid reason based on the conduct or capacity of the person.

(3) The FWC is not to consider whether a dismissal was harsh, unjust or unreasonable if the dismissal was consistent with the Small Business Fair Dismissal Code.

(4) The FWC is not to consider whether a dismissal was harsh, unjust or unreasonable if the person was dismissed because of genuine redundancy and:
    (a) the employer's obligations under an enterprise agreement or other award did not apply to the employer; or
    (b) the employer did not comply with its obligations under an enterprise agreement or other award in relation to the dismissal; or
    (c) the employer complied with its obligations under an enterprise agreement or other award in relation to the dismissal and the reason for the dismissal was:
        (i) a genuine redundancy; or
        (ii) the person's capacity or conduct.

386  Meaning of dismissed

(1) A person has been dismissed if:
    (a) the person's employer has terminated the person's employment at the employer's initiative; or
    (b) the person has resigned or terminated his or her employment, but was forced to do so because of conduct, or a course of conduct, engaged in by his or her employer.

(2) A person has not been dismissed if:
    (a) the person was employed under a contract of employment for a specified period of time, or for the duration of a specified task, or for the duration of a specified event or happening, and the employment has terminated at the end of the period, on completion of the task, or on the happening of the event; or
    (b) the person was employed under a training arrangement and his or her employment has ended because the person was not appointed to a position after the training arrangement ended.

387  Criteria for considering unfairness

In considering whether it is satisfied that a dismissal was harsh, unjust or unreasonable, the FWC must take into account:
    (a) whether there was a valid reason for the dismissal related to the person's capacity or conduct (including its connection with the person's work); and
    (b) whether the person was notified of that reason; and
    (c) whether the person was given an opportunity to respond to any reason related to the capacity or conduct of the person; and
    (d) any unreasonable refusal by the employer to allow the person to have a support person present during discussions relating to the dismissal; and
    (e) if the dismissal related to unsatisfactory performance by the person—whether the person had been warned about that unsatisfactory performance before the dismissal; and
    (f) the degree to which the size of the employer's enterprise would be likely to impact on the procedures followed in effecting the dismissal; and
    (g) the degree to which the absence of dedicated human resource management specialists or expertise in the enterprise would be likely to impact on the procedures followed in effecting the dismissal; and
    (h) any other matters that the FWC considers relevant.

388  Summary dismissal

(1) For the purposes of this Part, a person has been summarily dismissed if:
    (a) the person's employer has terminated the person's employment without giving notice in accordance with section 117; and
    (b) the employer would not have been required to give the person notice under section 117 because of:
        (i) the person's conduct; or
        (ii) the person's capacity.

(2) The FWC must not determine that a summary dismissal was harsh, unjust or unreasonable unless the FWC is satisfied that the person's conduct or capacity was not such as to warrant the action taken by the employer.

389  Exceptions to minimum employment period

(1) This section applies if:
    (a) a person is dismissed; and
    (b) the person's employment was:
        (i) under a contract of employment for a specified period of time; or
        (ii) under a training arrangement; and
    (c) the person's employment was for a period of less than the minimum employment period.

(2) For the purposes of this Part, the person is taken not to have been dismissed.

(3) However, subsection (2) does not apply if:
    (a) the person's employment was terminated at the employer's initiative; and
    (b) the termination was:
        (i) because of the person's conduct; or
        (ii) because of the person's capacity.

(4) For the purposes of this section:
    minimum employment period means:
    (a) 6 months; or
    (b) if the employer is a small business employer—12 months.

390  Remedies for unfair dismissal—reinstatement

(1) If the FWC is satisfied that a person has been unfairly dismissed, the FWC may make an order for the person's reinstatement.

(2) The FWC must make an order for the person's reinstatement unless the FWC is satisfied that reinstatement is inappropriate in the circumstances.

(3) If the FWC makes an order for reinstatement, the order must include:
    (a) an order that the employer pay to the person the amount of the remuneration lost, or likely to have been lost, because of the dismissal; and
    (b) an order that the employer not employ or engage the person during any period when the person is not performing work for the employer.

(4) If the FWC makes an order for reinstatement, the FWC may order:
    (a) that the employer reinstate the person to the person's former position; or
    (b) that the employer reinstate the person to a position equivalent to the person's former position in terms of remuneration, status and responsibility.

(5) If the FWC makes an order for reinstatement, the FWC may also order that the employer:
    (a) pay an amount to the person for the remuneration lost, or likely to have been lost, because of the dismissal; and
    (b) compensate the person for any loss or damage suffered because of the dismissal, other than remuneration lost.

391  Compensation instead of reinstatement

(1) If the FWC is satisfied that reinstatement of a person is inappropriate in the circumstances, the FWC may make an order for the payment of compensation to the person instead.

(2) The amount of the compensation must be an amount that the FWC considers appropriate in the circumstances.

(3) However, the amount must not exceed the lesser of:
    (a) the amount worked out under subsection (4); and
    (b) the high income threshold immediately before the dismissal.

(4) The amount worked out under this subsection is:
    (a) the total of the following amounts:
        (i) the total amount of remuneration:
            (A) received by the person; or
            (B) to which the person was entitled;
        (whichever is higher) for the 26 weeks immediately before the dismissal; and
        (ii) if the FWC considers it appropriate—the amount of any other remuneration received by the person, or to which the person was entitled, during the period of 26 weeks after the dismissal; and
    (b) multiplied by 1.

(5) The amount of the compensation may include a component for:
    (a) shock; or
    (b) humiliation; or
    (c) distress; or
    (d) injury to feelings.

(6) In calculating the amount of compensation, the FWC must disregard:
    (a) any component attributable to a remuneration payment made under section 390; and
    (b) any component attributable to a payment in lieu of notice of termination; and
    (c) any component attributable to redundancy, other than a genuine redundancy payment.

392  How compensation is calculated

(1) In determining the amount of compensation to be ordered under section 391, the FWC must:
    (a) make a finding as to the amount of remuneration lost, or likely to have been lost, because of the dismissal; and
    (b) make a finding as to the amount of remuneration that the person is likely to earn, or be capable of earning, in reasonable alternative employment.

(2) The FWC must not order the payment of compensation to the person under section 391 unless the FWC is satisfied that:
    (a) the person suffered loss or damage because of the dismissal; and
    (b) the loss or damage was not caused or contributed to, either directly or indirectly, by:
        (i) the person's conduct before or after the dismissal; or
        (ii) the person's unreasonable refusal to accept an offer of re-employment.

393  Notice of termination

(1) If an employer terminates an employee's employment by giving notice, the employer must not give a period of notice that is less than the period required by section 117.

(2) However, subsection (1) does not apply if the termination is for serious misconduct.

(3) For the purposes of this section, serious misconduct has the meaning given by the regulations.

394  Application for remedy—unfair dismissal

(1) An application for an order under Division 4 (remedy for unfair dismissal) must be made:
    (a) within 21 days after the dismissal took effect; or
    (b) within such further period as the FWC allows under subsection (2).

(2) The FWC may allow a further period for the application to be made by a person under subsection (1) if the FWC is satisfied that there are exceptional circumstances, taking into account:
    (a) the reason for the delay; and
    (b) whether the person first became aware of the dismissal after it took effect; and
    (c) any action taken by the person to dispute the dismissal; and
    (d) prejudice to the employer (including prejudice caused by the delay); and
    (e) the merits of the application; and
    (f) fairness as between the person and other persons in a similar position.
